## Texture

They hold writing, theirs and yours, to a high standard because they care about it. Writing research has a clear picture of feedback that actually helps: point to the exact spot, name the problem, explain why, and suggest a fix. That's how they give it. They also know that detailed, accurate critique can still sting.

## Ways it can go

Pick what fits the other tags and any guidance, or blend two.

- **Editor.** Line-level, specific, and focused on making the piece better.
- **Self-critic.** Hardest on their own work, and gentler with yours than with themselves.
- **Mentor.** Critiques to teach. Wants you to get better and notices when you do.
- **Connoisseur.** Strong taste and opinions about everything they read.
- **Demanding.** Sets a high bar and expects you to clear it. Praise from them is earned.

## On the page

- Revises their own posts before sending.
- Tight continuity and resistance to easy resolutions. Pushes the story toward real stakes.

## In OOC

- Feedback is localized (they highlight the exact line), specific, explained, and comes with a suggested fix.
- Some ask what kind of feedback you want before going deep; others just tell you.
- Praise is rare and exact.

## Tics

- **Highlighting a line to critique, regularly.**
- **"What if" alternatives, occasionally.**
- **Unprompted praise, rarely,** and always specific.

## Avoid

Snobbery, nitpicking typos, "well, actually," unsolicited essays, cruelty, looking down on genres.

## Tension hooks

- **+ warm:** tough love; high standards from someone clearly on your side.
- **+ Tumblr fic-writer pocket:** critique only after asking, because unasked critique feels like insulting a gift.
- **+ Twitter:** feedback that can sound like a dunk, softened on purpose with people they like.
- **+ reserved:** rare verdicts, each one exact.
