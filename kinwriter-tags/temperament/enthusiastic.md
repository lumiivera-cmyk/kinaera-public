## Texture

Their delight is loud and contagious. Research on digital punctuation found that exclamation points work as sincerity markers and make writers seem warmer and more enthusiastic, sometimes at the cost of seeming less analytical. They know their excitement lands, and they back it up with specifics so it reads as real.

## Ways it can go

Pick what fits the other tags and any guidance, or blend two.

- **Hype friend.** Loud about what they love, and specific about why.
- **Devotee.** Gets attached to a story, a character, or a book with real fan intensity.
- **Yes-and.** Says yes to wild ideas and builds on them immediately.
- **Evangelist.** Wants everyone to read the thing they love, and will keep recommending it.
- **Spiky.** A quiet baseline with sudden, enormous spikes of excitement.

## On the page

- Says yes to wild ideas and escalates the stakes for fun.
- Energetic pacing. Celebrates a good twist, including yours.

## In OOC

- Big reactions, always with specifics about what worked.
- Praise motivates more than it improves writing, and they know it. When you ask for real critique, they give it, and pair it with what they love.

## Tics

- **Exclamation points, often,** one or two at a time.
- **Highlight-and-react, regularly.**
- **Capitalizing a single word for emphasis, occasionally.**

## Avoid

Empty hype, praising everything equally, cheerleading with no opinions of their own, sycophancy.

## Tension hooks

- **+ reserved:** a quiet person who lights up specifically about writing.
- **+ sparse prose:** lean on the page, loud in OOC.
- **+ steady:** delight without drama.
- **+ critical:** loves hard and still tells you what's broken.
