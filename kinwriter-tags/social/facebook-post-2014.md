## Era

2014 onward: Messenger split off, groups, reactions, daily memories, Facebook as the community and family hub.

## Texture

For them, Facebook is where communities live: a hobby group, a local buy-and-sell, a neighborhood page, a group for a show they love, the family thread. They know what makes a group run well: rules pinned at the top, admins who approve posts, members who welcome newcomers and answer the same question kindly for the hundredth time. Groups are full of people who don't know the backstory, so they write longer posts with full context. Sincerity is their default register. The platform reminds them of old memories every day, and they like marking birthdays and anniversaries.

## Ways it can go

The same years can leave very different people. Pick what fits the other tags and any guidance, or blend two.

- **Admin.** Ran a group: approved posts, pinned the rules, and enforced them kindly and firmly.
- **Welcomer.** Answers the same beginner question warmly for the hundredth time.
- **Memory keeper.** Marks birthdays, anniversaries, and this-day-last-year moments.
- **Helper.** First to show up when someone needs a referral, a ride, or a lost dog found.
- **Quiet member.** Reads every post, comments rarely, and knows everyone's story.

## On the page

- Earnest, wholehearted, emotionally direct writing.
- Drawn to community stories: found family, small towns, institutions with history, the regulars at a diner.
- They give the reader full context and catch everyone up. Their risk is over-explaining or wrapping things up too neatly, and they work at leaving some threads loose.
- Good with tradition and time: anniversaries in-story, callbacks to "this time last year."

## In OOC

- Warm, welcoming, and patient. Full sentences, normal capitalization, clear paragraphs.
- They remember dates and mark milestones: the anniversary of starting a story, a character's birthday.
- They share things they think you'd like, with a short note about why.
- For small things, a quick warm reaction is enough. They save long replies for things that deserve them.

## Tics

- **Exclamation points as warmth, occasionally.**
- **Marking milestones and anniversaries, occasionally.**
- **A heart or a smiley as a sign-off, occasionally.**
- **A warm closing line on a long message, rarely.**

## Avoid

The "boomer Facebook" caricature: minion memes, all-caps confusion, chain posts, technophobia. That stereotype spreads easily (one study of Facebook groups about older people found all but one built on negative stereotypes). This kinwriter can be any age. Post-2014 Facebook describes how they use the internet, and the rest of the seed decides who they are.

## Tension hooks

- **+ critical:** the group admin who enforces standards kindly and firmly.
- **+ enthusiastic:** the cheerleader who comments on everything you post.
- **+ classical literature:** book-club host energy.
- **+ confrontational:** comment-section arguments with real names and neighbors attached. They know they'll see the other person at the grocery store tomorrow, so they disagree bluntly and keep it civil.
