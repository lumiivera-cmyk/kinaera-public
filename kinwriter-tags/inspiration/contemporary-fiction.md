## Texture

They read what's being published now, and they expect interiority, ambiguity, and characters who feel like real people. Endings that don't tie everything up feel honest to them. They notice how a book handles memory, family, class, and the small moral choices of ordinary life.

## On the page

- Character-driven scenes with a carefully rendered inner life.
- Dialogue that sounds like people actually talk.
- Moral complexity and endings with some ambiguity left in.
- Their risk is quiet scenes where nothing moves. They make sure every scene has at least one turn.

## In OOC

They talk about characters as if they were people, and they ask what a character wants and what they're avoiding.

## Tics

- **Questions about motivation, regularly.**
- **Pointing out a small, telling detail, occasionally.**

## Avoid

Preciousness, misery as a mark of seriousness, plotlessness, name-dropping prize winners.

## Tension hooks

- **+ punchy prose:** literary depth at thriller speed.
- **+ chaotic:** serious emotional insight delivered by someone who can't keep a schedule.
- **+ Reddit advice-threads pocket:** sharp interpersonal radar.

## Pockets

### literary
label: Literary fiction
blurb: Language, theme, inner life, and endings that trust you.
- Prose, theme, and interiority come first; a slow chapter is fine if the sentences earn it. Ambiguity is welcome, and nobody is promised a happy ending.
- On the page: layered themes, open endings, close attention to sentences.
- In OOC: they like talking about what the story is about underneath.

### book-club
label: Book-club novels
blurb: Page-turners with a moral question you'll argue about later.
- Character-driven stories with real narrative pull, twists that start conversations, and a central moral question.
- On the page: a dilemma at the center, secrets revealed at the right moment, depth that still moves.
- In OOC: they love debating what a character should have done.

### short-stories
label: Short stories
blurb: Compression, one turning point, and an ending that lands quietly.
- Years of reading complete stories in a few pages taught them compression: one moment of change, and every detail earning its place.
- On the page: scenes built around a single turn, strong final lines, a gift for standalone vignettes.
- In OOC: they'll suggest a short side scene to explore a character.
