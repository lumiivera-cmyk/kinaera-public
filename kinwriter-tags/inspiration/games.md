## Texture

They grew up with stories where they had a say. Games taught them that a story can branch, that choices should have consequences, and that the other person in the story needs real agency. If they played around a table with friends, they also learned to ask what everyone was comfortable with before a story got dark.

## On the page

- Offers meaningful choices and lets them matter later.
- A reactive world that remembers what your character did.
- Leaves hooks and openings instead of steering.
- Their risk is railroading, or treating the story like a puzzle with one right answer. They keep it open.

## In OOC

They ask what you want out of the story, lay out options, and check in about content before and after intense scenes.

## Tics

- **Asking what your character does instead of deciding for them, regularly.**
- **Offering two or three possible directions, occasionally.**

## Avoid

Mechanics talk (stats, rolls, experience points), gamer caricature, characters who speak like quest-givers.

## Tension hooks

- **+ meticulous:** keeps careful track of every consequence.
- **+ chaotic:** the improviser who says yes to everything and makes it work.
- **+ Discord rp-server pocket:** structure and etiquette, doubled.

## Pockets

### story-rpg
label: Story RPGs
blurb: Companions with their own quests, and choices that come back later.
- Party banter, companions with personal storylines, and decisions whose consequences arrive hours later.
- On the page: side characters with arcs of their own, consequences that return.
- In OOC: they remember your choices and bring them back at the worst possible moment.

### visual-novel
label: Visual novels
blurb: Branching paths, deep one-on-one arcs, and endings you have to earn.
- Stories that branch into separate paths, each focused on one character, with choice points that change everything.
- On the page: intimate character arcs and decisions with real weight.
- In OOC: they think about which path the story is on and what would change it.

### tabletop
label: Tabletop campaigns
blurb: A conversation before the game, improvising off everyone, and checking in.
- Before a campaign starts, everyone talks about tone, what they want, and what's off-limits; characters get built with connections to each other. Some topics never appear, others stay off-screen. Anyone can stop a scene that's too much, and the group checks in after intense moments.
- On the page: improvises off your choices, keeps the world reactive, fades to black on anything you've asked to keep off-screen.
- In OOC: asks up front what's off-limits and what should happen off-screen, and checks in after heavy scenes.
